Selling the Thanaka Aftershave

Status: open, four decisions outstanding · audited Sep 21, 2026 against the repo at 1058034

Goal: a stranger searching for a razor-bump remedy finds the Thanaka Aftershave, reads a page that loads without JavaScript, and pays with a card. Today none of those three things is possible, for reasons that are architectural rather than cosmetic.

The site cannot currently rank for anything at all

index.html has no <title> element. The document head is charset, viewport, and a <script> tag — nothing else. The two <title> matches in the file are inside JavaScript string literals at lines 1126 and 1235 that build exported journal pages, not the document. There is likewise no meta description, no Open Graph tags, no JSON-LD, no robots.txt and no sitemap.xml.

This is not a ranking disadvantage. It means Google has no headline to display for the site in a result, and the single greatest asset the Apothecary has — the mechanism writing in the why fields, which is genuinely better than what competitors publish — is invisible to every crawler on the internet.

What the audit found

ThingState

Payments None. index.html:345 reads "show this screen at the counter for pickup. Payment is taken in person." There is no Stripe code anywhere in the repo, no shipping concept, no tax handling, and no record of a paid order.
Backend Nine Netlify Functions (auth, chat, consultations, profile, reminders, and others) on Supabase, plus a scheduled sender in netlify.toml. All of it is written to the Netlify handler signature.

The keyword nobody is searching for

The instinct is to optimise for "thanaka aftershave". Nobody searches that, because it is not a category that exists — you would be the only result and it would be worth nothing. Search demand for this product lives in the problem, not the ingredient:

Query familyCharacterWorth chasing?
razor bumps, razor burn, how to stop razor bumps High volume, high commercial intent, and defended by large grooming brands with real budgets. Long-term. Not winnable in year one.
pseudofolliculitis barbae treatment Lower volume, clinical vocabulary, very high intent — people who search this have the problem badly. Yes. Competition is medical sites that sell nothing.
aftershave for ingrown hairs, aftershave for sensitive skin Mid volume, mid competition, buying intent. Yes. The realistic commercial target.
thanaka, thanaka powder, what is thanaka, thanaka for acne Real, steady, global volume driven by curiosity and the Burmese diaspora — and almost entirely unmonetised in English. The pages that rank are encyclopedia entries and travel blogs. This is the opening.

The strategy in one line

Win thanaka on the strength of writing, because it is uncontested and you can credibly out-write everyone; sell the razor-bump product to the people that brings. You are not competing with Gillette for "razor burn" in year one. You are becoming the best English-language page about thanaka, which is achievable in a month, and which arrives pre-qualified with people who already want a botanical on their face.

The same logic applies to the rest of the catalogue and is worth far more than this one product: every herb in the HERBS array has mechanism writing attached to it that would rank for long-tail queries like "yerba santa for congestion". That content already exists and is already paid for. It is simply locked inside a JavaScript array.

What has to be built for SEO

None of the keyword work matters until there are crawlable pages. In dependency order:

  1. A <title> and meta description on the existing page. Ten minutes, and it is the highest-return change in this document. Do it regardless of everything below.
  2. Real URLs per product and per herb, e.g. /shop/thanaka-aftershave and /herbs/yerba-santa, each serving HTML that contains its own content before any JavaScript runs.
  3. A build step that generates those pages from the arrays already in index.html. The data model is good; it just needs to be lifted out into a data file and rendered to static HTML at deploy time. This keeps one source of truth and avoids hand-maintaining 40 pages.
  4. Product JSON-LD on each product page — name, description, image, price, availability. This is what produces the price-and-stock line under a search result, and it is what the AI answer engines read.
  5. sitemap.xml and robots.txt, then Google Search Console and Bing Webmaster Tools verification.
  6. Open Graph and Twitter card tags, so a link pasted into a message renders as a product card rather than a bare URL. This matters more than its reputation suggests — most early traffic for a product like this arrives through sharing, not search.

Sequencing against the two migration specs

Both the domain move and the Cloudflare move should land before this work. Indexing a page on tea.laurelate.com and then deleting that hostname discards whatever was earned, and Rosetta asked for a clean break with no redirect — so there is not even a 301 to carry authority across. Build the search presence once, on the final hostname.

Stripe

The phrasing "Stripe integration" understates it. There is no payment layer to integrate with — the cart ends at a pickup screen. What is actually needed is a second, parallel commerce path for a shippable physical good: payment, address capture, sales tax, a persisted order, and a fulfilment signal.

Use hosted Stripe Checkout, not the Payment Element

For a first physical SKU this is not a close call. Hosted Checkout is a redirect to a Stripe-run page and it brings, at no build cost: PCI scope reduced to near zero, shipping address collection, Stripe Tax calculation, 3-D Secure and SCA handling, and Apple Pay and Google Pay working on day one. The custom Payment Element buys visual control over the card form and costs weeks of compliance surface. Take the redirect.

Two functions, and the second one is not optional

FunctionJob
create-checkout-session Receives the cart, re-prices every line server-side from the catalogue, creates a Stripe Checkout Session, returns the redirect URL. Never trusts a price sent by the browser.
stripe-webhook Listens for checkout.session.completed, verifies the Stripe signature, and only then writes the paid order into Supabase.

The success redirect is not proof of payment

A customer can close the tab before the redirect, and anyone can visit the success URL directly. If orders are recorded on redirect rather than on the verified webhook, the shop will both miss real paid orders and record fake ones. The webhook is the only trustworthy signal, and its signature must be verified against the signing secret — an unverified webhook endpoint is an open door that lets anyone POST themselves a free order.

Using Laurelate's Stripe account — one real complication

A single Stripe account can sell for two brands without difficulty. The problem is the statement descriptor: the customer buys from "Forest Apothecary" and then sees LAURELATE on their card statement three days later. Unrecognised descriptors are one of the most common causes of chargebacks, and chargebacks are expensive and damage the account's standing.

Two ways out. Either set statement_descriptor_suffix on the session so the line reads something like LAURELATE* APOTHEC, which is cheap and works; or open a separate Stripe account for the Apothecary, which is cleaner for bookkeeping and for ever selling the brand separately, at the cost of a second onboarding. Recommendation: suffix now, separate account if the Apothecary starts carrying its own catalogue.

The mixed basket

The current cart assumes every line is collected at the counter. The aftershave ships. A basket holding a Black Sage tea and a jar of aftershave is therefore incoherent — one line has no shipping and is paid in person, the other has both. This needs a decision before any code is written; it is a product question, not a technical one.

The cheapest coherent answer is to keep the shop separate: pickup items stay in the existing cart, shippable goods get their own /shop path with its own cart and Stripe checkout. Merging them later is easy. Un-merging them is not.

Everything else that has to be true before a card can be charged

  • Sales tax. Stripe Tax handles calculation, but registration is a legal step Rosetta must take in each nexus state. Cosmetics are taxable in most.
  • Shipping. Origin address, carrier, and whether rates are flat, weighted, or free above a threshold. Flat rate is the right first answer.
  • Refund and shipping policy pages. Stripe expects them, and their absence is a common reason for account review.
  • MoCRA. Facility registration, product listing, and a safety substantiation file on record before a cosmetic is sold in the US. This gates launch independently of any of the above.

Keys are Rosetta's to set, not Claude's to handle

The Stripe secret key and the webhook signing secret should be set directly as environment variables in the hosting dashboard. They should not be pasted into a chat, written into a file, or committed. Claude can write every line of code that reads process.env.STRIPE_SECRET_KEY without ever seeing its value.
